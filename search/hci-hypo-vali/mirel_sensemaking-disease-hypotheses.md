# Mirel & Görg: Scientists' Sensemaking when Hypothesizing about Disease Mechanisms

## One Sentence
A 2014 BMC Bioinformatics observational study of one biomedical scientist over six months that models the six-stage sensemaking workflow for generating disease mechanism hypotheses from expression data and derives eight visualization design guidelines.

## More Sentences
The study employed longitudinal observation (six months, one researcher, heart failure expression data) combined with protocol analysis to document how interactive visualization integrates with literature review, data exploration, and hypothesis formulation in a real scientific workflow. Surprisingly, direct visualization tool use accounted for only 13% of total analysis time; the rest involved literature synthesis and knowledge building. The authors abstracted a six-stage sensemaking model: characterizing genes, contextualizing in biological events, background reading, explanatory reading, schematizing/hypothesizing, and write-up composition.

## Key Points

### Visualization as a Minority Activity
> "Only 3 weeks of 6 months involved direct visualization tool use" — the rest devoted to literature-driven knowledge building.

This finding challenges tool-centric approaches to hypothesis validation support and suggests HCI systems must integrate with broader sensemaking workflows, not replace them.

### Six-Stage Sensemaking Model
The workflow model extends SDDS to domain-realistic conditions, where hypothesis formulation (stage 5) is preceded by multi-week knowledge-building phases — a much slower, more iterative cycle than controlled experiments suggest.

### Eight Design Guidelines
Guidelines address filtering, data interoperability, network layouts, multi-scale analysis, multiple data perspectives, external representation construction, document annotation, and investigation history — a practical design agenda for bioinformatics VA tools supporting hypothesis validation.

## Other Notes
Authors: Barbara Mirel, Carsten Görg. Published in BMC Bioinformatics, Vol. 15, Article 117 (2014). Naturalistic observational study (n=1 scientist, deep case study methodology). The small sample is a limitation; the granularity compensates. Adjacent to HCI, published in bioinformatics, but methodologically aligned with HCI design research.

## Take-Away
Grounds hypothesis validation in realistic scientific work practice rather than controlled lab tasks, revealing that tool design must support the full cognitive cycle — including extended knowledge-building phases — not just the final moment of data-versus-model comparison.
