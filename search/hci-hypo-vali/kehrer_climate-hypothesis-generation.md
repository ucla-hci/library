# Kehrer et al.: Hypothesis Generation in Climate Research with Interactive Visual Data Exploration

## One Sentence
A 2008 IEEE TVCG paper demonstrating that coordinated multiple-views visualization of multivariate climate data enables climate researchers to "steer" hypothesis generation for subsequent statistical evaluation — an early example of tool-supported hypothesis production in the SDDS experiment-space loop.

## More Sentences
The system applied to two atmospheric datasets derives higher-order information such as linear trends and signal-to-noise ratios, allowing researchers to identify atmospheric regions sensitive to climate change. Interactive exploration narrows the parameter space before formal statistical tests are applied, meaning the tool mediates between exploratory visual hypothesis generation and confirmatory statistical validation. The authors note the approach generalizes to other multivariate scientific applications.

## Key Points

### Visual Exploration as Hypothesis-Space Narrowing
The tool uses coordinated views to narrow the experiment space, enabling researchers to identify candidate hypotheses (sensitive climate indicators) before committing to costly statistical tests.

### Integration into the Dual-Space Loop
The workflow explicitly couples interactive hypothesis generation (visual exploration) with downstream statistical validation — a concrete implementation of the SDDS search → test → evaluate cycle.

### Steered Discovery
> "Interactive visual data exploration of large amounts of multi-variate and time-dependent climate data enables the steered generation of promising hypotheses for subsequent statistical evaluation."

## Other Notes
Authors: Johannes Kehrer, Florian Ladstädter, Philipp Muigg, Helmut Doleisch, Andrea Steiner, Helwig Hauser. Published in IEEE TVCG 14(6), 2008. An early foundational paper — now 17 years old. Relevance is primarily historical (early example of tool-mediated dual-space workflow), and in the *hypothesis generation* direction more than validation. [abstract only]

## Take-Away
Important historical precedent showing that visualization tools have long been used to bridge the hypothesis generation–validation divide; situates current work in a longer research trajectory.
