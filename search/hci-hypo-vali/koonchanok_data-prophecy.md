# Koonchanok et al.: Data Prophecy

## One Sentence
A CHI 2021 study finding that eliciting analysts' prior beliefs before showing them visualizations makes them more attentive to discrepancies with their hypotheses but simultaneously reduces exploratory interactions.

## More Sentences
Participants were asked to make predictions ("prophecies") about data patterns before viewing visualizations, externalizing hypotheses as a form of pre-registration. Those who articulated priors were more likely to notice when data contradicted their mental models, suggesting belief elicitation activates a hypothesis-checking cognitive mode. However, this came at the cost of breadth: belief-elicited participants made fewer exploratory discoveries, indicating a trade-off between confirmatory vigilance and open-ended exploration.

## Key Points

### Belief Elicitation as Hypothesis Externalization
Asking users to predict outcomes before data is shown operationalizes the "check hypothesis" process in SDDS — making implicit prior beliefs into explicit testable claims.

### Confirmation-Exploration Trade-off
> "Participants were more likely to attend to discrepancies between their mental models and the data, but were also less likely to engage in exploratory interactions."

### Design Implications
The trade-off suggests that hypothesis validation affordances may suppress the exploratory phase — a finding with implications for when in an analysis workflow confirmatory interaction should be introduced.

## Other Notes
Authors: Ratanond Koonchanok, Gauri Tawde, Gokul Narayanasamy, Khairi Reda. Directly precedes the more controlled experiment in Visual Belief Elicitation (CHI 2023) by the same group. The "prophecy" metaphor anticipates later work on prior elicitation interfaces.

## Take-Away
Establishes empirically that surfacing hypotheses before data viewing changes analytic behavior — a key finding for designing HCI systems that scaffold the hypothesis validation phase without eliminating exploratory discovery.
