# Koonchanok et al.: Visual Belief Elicitation Reduces False Discovery

## One Sentence
A CHI 2023 controlled experiment demonstrating that prompting analysts to specify their expected data pattern before viewing a visualization yields 21% more correct inferences and 12% fewer false discoveries.

## More Sentences
Two experiments exposed participants to both spurious and "true" scatterplots and measured their ability to identify the correct data-generating model. The prior elicitation intervention — asking participants to "paint" the expected pattern before the data was revealed — produced reliable improvements across varied sample characteristics. Additional interventions (highlighting counterevidence, displaying sample uncertainty) did not add significant benefit beyond the prior elicitation alone.

## Key Points

### Lightweight Intervention, Reliable Effect
> "Participants who underwent prior belief elicitation made 21% more correct inferences along with 12% fewer false discoveries."

### Broad Generalizability
The benefit held across a "variety of sample characteristics," suggesting the mechanism — externalizing prior hypotheses before evaluation — is robust rather than context-dependent.

### Mechanism: Hypothesis Anchoring
Eliciting a prior creates a hypothesis anchor that sensitizes analysts to confirming vs. disconfirming evidence, reducing the tendency to accept noise as signal.

## Other Notes
Authors: Ratanond Koonchanok, Gauri Yatindra Tawde, Gokul Ragunandhan Narayanasamy, Shalmali Walimbe, Khairi Reda. Methodologically rigorous follow-up to Data Prophecy (CHI 2021). The effect size is moderate but consistent — design teams can implement belief elicitation as a low-cost, evidence-based feature.

## Take-Away
Provides quantitative evidence that making hypothesis validation explicit (via prior elicitation) improves inferential accuracy in visual data analysis — directly actionable for HCI system design in scientific contexts.
