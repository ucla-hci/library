# Key Points


### What’s trust in automation

> People’s attitude regarding the ability of automation to perform a task has been referred to as their trust in the automation …
> 

### The three stages of trust (on anything)

1. *Predictability*: “whether behavior is consistent and desirable … constraints on behavior are known”
2. *Dependability*: “the degree to which it is possible to rely on a person”
3. *Faith*: “… extrapolates trust into the future and new situations”

### Compliance vs. reliance

The use of the term “reliance” here is quite narrow and deviates from that in “overreliance”.

- Compliance: “the tendency to perform an action when the system instructs the user to perform the action”
- Reliance: “the tendency to refrain from performing an action when the warning system does not indicate that is is necessary”

### Trust should be considered as an intervening variable

> … one benefit of considering trust as an intervening variable, rather than as a behavior, is that it places trust in the context of many other factors that affect behavior.
> 

# Other Notes


### Two schools of study on trust

1. Trust as a psychological entity that is latent and not directly observable but nonetheless influence behavior
2. Trust as an abstraction that describes behavior of a person acts trustingly (i.e., without being grounded in such behaviors one cannot speak about trust)

### Psychological reactance

> … a reaction to situations in which people feel their autonomy is threatened. In these situations they may reaffirm their freedom and autonomy by doing the opposite of what they were instructed to do.
> 

This phenomenon is often observed in various contexts, such as in marketing (when aggressive sales tactics backfire), in parenting (when strict rules lead to rebellious behavior), and in politics (when too much control leads to resistance).
