# Key Points


### What is metacognition

> … the psychological ability to monitor and control one’s thoughts and behavior …
> 

The two processes when conducting a task

- **Object-level** processes “reflect the basic cognitive work of perceiving, remembering, classifying, deciding, and so on”;
- **Meta-level** processes monitor those object-level processes to assess their functioning (e.g., assessing how well one grasped the gist of a text) and allocate resources appropriately (e.g., deciding to re-read the text).

### Metacognitive abilities: monitoring and control

**Monitoring** abilities “involve the assessment of one’s own thinking”, relevant to GenAI—

- *Self-awareness*: “recognize one’s own thoughts, emotions, and actions, as well as how these factors influence cognition”
- *adjustment of confidence*: “one’s self-assessment of one’s cognitive abilities and their application to tasks”

**Control** abilities “directly guide one’s own thinking”, relevant to GenAI—

- *Metacognitive flexibility*: “the ability to adaptively shift cognitive strategies when encountering new information, when realizing that a current strategy isn’t effective, or when the demands of the task change”
- *Task decomposition*: “breaking down a task into concrete, actionable subtasks or steps”

### What makes it uniquely difficult to evaluate GenAI output

- **The extensiveness of GenAI’s novel content output**, e.g., entire email, presentation, software.
    - “Ackerman [2] found that people’s internal confidence threshold for solving reasoning problems decreases as the required effort increases; that is, ‘*when problems took longer to solve, participants appeared to compromise on their confidence criterion, and were willing to provide solutions with less confidence*’”
- **The relative ease of novel content generation**,
    - One indicator is processing fluency, e.g., “answers to various problems are judged as more correct simply if they are displayed faster to participants after problem descriptions”.
- **Multiple, non-intuitive failure modes of GenAI**.
    - Users don’t know what kinds of error to expect from GenAI
    - Difficult to trace what causes an error
- **Obtaining appropriate measures for confidence adjustment**
    - Unlike “the number of errors a user correctly detected in the output”, “… the quality of generated content and its uses may be more difficult to evaluate objectively”

### Addressing the metacognitive demands of GenAI

**Overall strategies**

1. “*improving users’ metacognition* via metacognitive support strategies that can be integrated into GenAI systems, and
2. “*reducing the metacognitive demand* of GenAI systems by designing task-appropriate approaches to explainability and customizability.”

**1. Improving user metacognition**

- *Planning* — “a task-oriented metacognitive strategy entailing both the definition of clear goals (i.e., self-awareness) and devising a comprehensive approach for achieving them by breaking them down into smaller, manageable steps (i.e., task decomposition).”
- *Self-evaluation*—”enabling users to reflect on their knowledge, strategies, and performance, and their respective level of confidence in these”, “For example, simple textual prompts promoting self-awareness significantly decreased participants’ susceptibility to incorrect (albeit realistic) information”
- *Self-management*—”involves the strategic management of variables like time, setting, and workflow …”
1. **Reducing metacognitive demands**
- *Explainability*—”can surface the information necessary for adjusting confidence in prompting, output evaluation, and automation strategy.”
- *Customizability*—”can support metacognition, particularly for more advanced users”, e.g., allowing expert users to change the temperature setting.

# Other Notes


### Metacognitive knowledge vs. experiences

- **Metacognitive knowledge is explicit**, about “people’s conscious understanding of aspects like their strategies (e.g., memory strategies), reading abilities, decision-making, and beliefs”;
- **Meta cognitive experiences can be implicit**, “occurring without our direct intention or awareness”, such as one’s feelings of familiarity, misunderstanding, or “process fluency”, i.e., feeling the ease of processing information.
