This paper presents Dream Lens—a tool for viewing and exploring a large set of generative 3D models.

# More Sentences


# Key Points


### The "1 dot = 1 design" principle

> ... a constant mapping of "1 dot = 1 design" is followed. That is, each dot in any of the controls in the property explorer represents a single design. As the cursor hovers over designs in one control, those designs are highlighted in all the other controls, and selections made in one control are immediately reflected in the others.
> 

### Both the input and output attributes can be used to explore different designs

> The single-attribute controllers are arranged in two columns, with the "Input Parameters" on the left, and the "Model Characteristics" on the right.
> 

### Finding a representative example from some range of values

> ... for each of the key values, the 10 closest models are found. Then, the centroid of these models is computed by averaging all of the attributes for these designs, and the most "representative" example of this group is calculated as the design with the lowest root mean square error (RMSE) from the centroid.
> 

### Showing the generative process is useful

> We found it useful to see what a design looked like a few iterations earlier, or a few iterations later.
> 

# Other Notes


### The use of tooltips

> When there are many designs, the images are quite small. To address this, a large tooltip is displayed to show an enlarged image of the design.
> 

### Stacking (overlaying) examples

> With this (stacked) view, users can directly see the differences in topology or geometry among a small set of models and when stacking a larger number of models it is possible to see regions of high and overlap, in addition to common features among the individual designs.
>
