### The historical origin of "scientific discovery as problem solving"
> the thesis that scientific discovery is problem solving writ large, and that it can be simulated by computer, is not completely novel. It goes back at least as far as a paper delivered in 1958 by Newell, Shaw, and Simon.

### The caveat that scientific discovery also involves problem finding & defining
> ... in much scientific inquiry---including some of the most important and challenging---the targets are less sharp ... finding problems and formulating them is as integral a part of the inquiry process as is solving them, once they have been found. And setting criteria of goal satisfaction, so that indefinite goals become definite, is an essential part of formulating problems.

### A more objective angle of looking at "scientific discovery as problem solving"
It's the subtasks that might share similar nature:
> ... the component processes of scientific discovery are not qualitatively distinct from the processes that have been observed in simpler problem-solving situations.

### What scientific discover boils down to
> Thus, for problem solving we postulate an information-processing system that creates problem representations and searches selectively through trees of intermediate situations, seeking the goal situation and using heuristics to guide its search.
