# Key Points


### Swanson’s A-B-C model

> … the model of discovery from public knowledge is the A-B-C model, where the connections of A-B and B-C are known, but the connection of A-C is unknown. Thus A-C has the potential to become a candidate hypothesis for domain experts to evaluate.
> 

Examples:

> … the connection between fish oil and Raynaud’s syndrome … magnesium and migraine … indomethacin and Alzheimer’s disease …
> 

### Kuhn’s paradigm-shift model

> … describes how science advances through a path of normal science, crisis, revolution, and new normal science.
> 

Opposing opinion:

> Critics argue that scientific change is often a lengthy process instead of a swift change as the paradigm-shift model suggests.
> 

### Small’s model

> … discoveries can be modeled as navigation between pairs of established experimental or theoretical findings.
> 

Example: the discovery of superconductivity in a type of ceramic materials connects the field of superconductivity (used to focus only on metals or metal alloys) and a class of ceramic compounds.

In essence, this seems the same thing as the A-B-C model

### Burt’s structural hole model

Burt's structural hole theory, developed by sociologist Ronald Burt, is a concept in social network analysis that describes gaps between non-redundant contacts within a social network. These gaps, or "structural holes," represent opportunities for individuals to act as brokers, connecting otherwise disconnected groups and facilitating the flow of information and resources across the network.

Interpreting this theory in SD:

> Structural holes in this sense span across patches of knowledge at different levels of self-organized structures, ranging from areas of research, fields of study, to disciplines.
> 

### This paper’s proposed theory

> Our theory states that a transformative discovery is made when a bridging connection is established between two ore more previously disconnected patches of knowledge.
If we represent knowledge in the form of networks, such bridging connections would be links between two or more disconnected networks or components of a network.
Such connections in networks can be computationally identified using the betweenness centrality.
> 

Betweenness centrality of a node measures how frequently it lies on the shortest paths that connect two nodes across a network.
