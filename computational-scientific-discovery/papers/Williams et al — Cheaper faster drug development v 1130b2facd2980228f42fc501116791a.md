# One Sentence


This paper describes Eve—a Robot Scientist focused on the target ID phase in drug discovery, which can automate the initial hit screening, iteratively conduct further “hit confirmation”, and compute the QSAR metric.

# Key Points


### Problem: limitation of existing screening techniques for hit ID

> Such mass screening is generally done in a brute-force and unintelligent way … As the *a priori* probability of any library compound being a hit is low, it is difficult to design an assay that does not have an appreciable number of false positives.
> 

### Why important to support early stages of DD?

> Preventing drug failures in late-stage development is an intrinsically very hard problem, as human biology is very complex.
> 

### What Eve can do

1. Library-screening mode: “… systematically tests each member from a large set of compounds against an assay in the standard brute-force way of conventional mass screening”
2. Hit-confirmation mode: “Eve re-assays the hit compounds using multiple repeats and titrations to reduce the probability of false positives”
3. QSAR-learning mode: “Eve executes cycles of statistics/machine learning that hypothesize QSARs, and tests these QSARs on new compounds”

# Other Notes


### What is QSAR (quantitative structure activity relationship)?

> … a function whose input is the structure of a compound, and whose output is the predicted activity on the assay. As the output is typically a real number, QSAR learning is generally a regression task.
>
