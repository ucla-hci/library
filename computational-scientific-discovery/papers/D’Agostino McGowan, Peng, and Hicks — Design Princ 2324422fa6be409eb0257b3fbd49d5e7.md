# One Sentence


The authors (i) analogize/draw parallels between statistical analysis authoring and design thinking and (ii) introduce 6 design principles for authoring data analyses.

# More Sentences


6 principles: 

- Data matching: Does the available data actually match the scientific question?
- Exhaustive: Has the analyst explored enough of the relevant analytic space?
- Skeptical: Does the analysis actively challenge its own conclusions?
- Second-order: Does the analysis reason about the analysis itself?
- Clarity: Can the consumer understand the analysis and its implications?
- Reproducible: Can someone reconstruct the analysis?

# Key Points


This paper establishes a perspective in statistics/statistics education around thinking about statistical analysis as a design process. This work contributes to a larger framing of statistics as a sensemaking process (i.e., Grolemund & Wickham). Scientific discovery is also a sensemaking process. Given that statistics is a key part of scientific discovery, it would be interesting to connect statistical analysis theories as part of the scientific discovery process. (The two literatures remain pretty disparate.)

# Other Notes


This paper (and the line of work on analytic design theory) is an example of applying “typical” HCI ideas (i.e., design) to a scientific process (i.e., statistical analysis). Importantly, this paper is written by statisticians, so if we, as HCI researchers, were to do this, we would likely do it differently.

# Take-Away

If we are to evaluate scientific discovery as individual processes (each leading to some specific findings, e.g., data sources and reasoning steps that lead to the discovery of a specific novel drug target), these principles feel more like heuristics to evaluate such processes.
