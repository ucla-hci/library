### Two types of expertise for scientific discovery
> ... (1) contributory expertise, expertise required to make a contribution to a field or discipline ... and (2) interactional expertise, socialised knowledge that includes socialisation into the practices of an expert group ... These forms of expertise are foten tacit, codified by "learning-by-doing," and augmented from project to project; therefore, they are difficult to measure and rarely documented in literature.

### The importance of interpersonal relationships
> Collectively, these studies argue that the key to collective intelligence is highly reliant on interpersonal relationships to drive team success.

### Lessons for the informal peer support project
The need for interactional expertise motivates the informal peer support project as the ability to seek/provide such support is part of the interactional expertise.

Love et al. show that advice and mentoring relationships overlap with scientific-productivity relationships, but their annual network surveys do not reveal how these ties form or operate in everyday interactions. Studying regular lab meetings can expose this missing mechanism: how members display difficulty, solicit or volunteer help, respond to support, and develop expectations about who is safe and useful to approach.

Informal peer support should therefore be understood not only as a transfer of technical or *contributory expertise*, but also as a practice through which members acquire *interactional expertise* and become recognized participants in the lab. Important distinctions include direct versus indirect requests, technical versus emotional or face-saving support, public meeting help versus private follow-up, and offers of help versus support that is actually taken up.

The paper motivates—but does not establish—a causal claim that interpersonal relationships drive productivity. Advice may enable collaboration, collaboration may create occasions for advice, or both may reflect shared projects, seniority, and time on the team. Our project can contribute by shifting the unit of analysis from annually reported relationships to observable support episodes and their consequences during recurring meetings.
