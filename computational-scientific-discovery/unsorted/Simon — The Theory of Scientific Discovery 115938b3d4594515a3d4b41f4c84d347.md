### The two sides of AI

1. Enabling computer programs to do things that humans would consider intelligent (if they were to do those things by themselves)
2. Understanding how the human mind does intelligent things (i.e., cognitive science)

### How come AI can advance without fully understanding how the brain works: an analogical explanation

Chemistry was able to develop its own theory for a long time without “really having any help or very much help from the physicist as to the underlying structures of atoms, the internal structure of atoms, and the elementary particles …”

### Scientific work is unstructured …

… compared to chess playing, which can be represented by well-structured data.

Simon calls it “ill-structured problem solving”:

# Take-Away


### The kind of science focused on in this paper

Overall, Simon’s notion of scientific discovery is mainly an inductive process: given a dataset of observation, find out the law that governs certain relationships between observed variables. Simon calls it “data-driven science”

Another type of activities (e.g., the discovery of penicillium), can be characterized as observing and noticing something unexpected that leads to a desirable result, which the scientist then tries to expand and scope it to the solving of a specific problem.
