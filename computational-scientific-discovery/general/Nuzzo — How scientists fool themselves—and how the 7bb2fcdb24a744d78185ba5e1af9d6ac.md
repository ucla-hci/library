# One Sentence



This article describes various kinds of biases that might influence scientists’ work.

# More Sentences



> Science is an ongoing race between our inventing ways to fool ourselves, and our inventing ways to avoid fooling ourselves
> 

— Saul Perlmutter, an astrophysicist at UC Berkeley

# Key Points



### Hypothesis myopia

> … investigators fixate on collecting evidence to support just one hypothesis; neglect to look for evidence against it; and fail to consider other explanations.
> 

### Asymmetric attention

> … sometimes known as disconfirmation bias, this happens when we give expected results a relatively free pass, but we rigorously check non-intuitive results.
> 

### Just-so storytelling

> … post-hoc stories can be concocted to justify anything and everything—and so end up truly explaining nothing
> 

Scientists tell stories (sometimes go back to change their hypotheses) that fit or match what the results are like.

### Solutions: consider competing hypotheses

> … explicitly considering competing hypotheses, and if possible working to develop experiments that can distinguish between them.
> 

Instead of cherry-picking the hypothesis you want to be true and prove it, try to prove the competing hypothesis to see if that’s true too …

### Solution: blind data analysis

The rationale:

> … researchers who do not know how close they are to desired results will be less likely to find what they are unconsciously looking for
> 

How to implement this: replace the actual data set with a fake one, apply the whole suite of methods on the fake data set; when finished, simply apply the same methods again on the real data set.

# Other Notes



### The impact of software

> … the widespread use of point-and-click data-analysis software has made it easy for researchers to sift through massive data sets without fully understanding the methods.

LLM is making this worse.
