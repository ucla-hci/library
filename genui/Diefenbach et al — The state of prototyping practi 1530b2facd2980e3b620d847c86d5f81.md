# Key Points

### Background of the double diamond model

> The double diamond published by the Design Council (2007) serves as a model to describe the phases of the design process
> 

> It builds upon the assumption that the design of a product is manifested in a design space. This includes all knowledge about the problem and possible solutions.
> 

> Divergent activities open the design space by gaining knowledge and pointing out alternatives. Convergent activities like synthesis and decision-making narrow it.
> 

### Mapping prototyping to the double diamond model

- Discover — “Prototypes are built to learn about the user and the context”
- Define — “Prototypes are built to validate assumptions and compare alternative ideas in terms of feasibility and user experience as well as for communication to decision makers”
- Develop — “Prototypes are used to explore and evaluate design decisions within a defined solution space referring to the design opportunity. Subsequent prototypes explore and validate the solution.”
- Deliver — “brings the product to the market … evolutionary prototypes are built for evaluation”

### Barrier: missing knowledge, skills or resources

> … such as people being reluctant to draw (which prevents the creation of early concept sketches and low fidelity prototyping).
> 

### Barrier: lack of communication

> … on a team level, such as lacking communication between internal prototyping stakeholders
> 

> lacking exploitation of prototyping as a communication tool
> 

### Barrier: lack of awareness of prototyping for different purposes

> lacking awareness of positioning in double diamond and matching particular prototyping methods to stakeholders.
> 

### Barrier: lack of awareness of full spectrum of prototyping methods

> too narrow spectrum of “prototypes” in use, e.g., focus on technologically advanced prototypes and testing of functions from a technical perspective, disregard of low fidelity methods and exploration of functions from a UX/experimental perspective.
>
