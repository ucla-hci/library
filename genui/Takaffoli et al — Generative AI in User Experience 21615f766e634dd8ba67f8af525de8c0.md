We conducted interviews with 24 UX practitioners from multiple companies and countries, with varying roles and seniority. # One Sentence

---

This paper reports findings from an interview study with UX practitioners regarding their use of generative AI:

1. There is a significant lack of GenAI company policies, with companies informally advising caution or leaving the responsibility to individual employees
2. UX teams lack team-wide GenAI practices. UX practitioners typically use GenAI individually, favoring writing-based tasks, but note limitations for design-focused activities, like wireframing and prototyping
3. UX practitioners call for better training on GenAI to enhance their abilities to generate effective prompts and evaluate output quality.

# Take-Away

---

### Potential limitation of participant demographics

> … 24 industry UX professionals … from eight different countries … varying levels of seniority, and served in different UX-related role, including designers, researchers, and managers
> 

It’d be complementarily deeper and more insightful if we could study UX practitioners coming from the same org.

### Empirically-known challenges of usingi GenAI in UX

- Writing effective GenAI prompts:
    - “spend more time on the right prompt instead of doing it on their own”
    - “… reduced efficiency, hurting the UX practitioner’s ability for critical thinking, and being unable to identify low-quality output due to not having sufficiently developed their own skills.”
- Assessing output quality
- Over-relying on GenAI

### Opportunities for Genux

- Support team-level activities. GenAI can become a tool to facilitate communication and catalyze collaboration amongst team members. For example, PM can use GenAI to create their envisioned UI, which then allows designers to work on and improve it.
- Like text and images, UX design is becoming another modality AI can generate.
