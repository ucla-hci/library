# Key Points

---

### Countering the major criticism

> … in many celebrated cases of discovery, the discoverer neither set the problem, which was already well known, nor provided a new representation for it.
> 

Examples of SD not about finding new problems:

- Kepler, Newton, Einstein, and Planck famously solved problems that others have found/defined, rather than finding their own problems

Example of SD not about finding new representations of problems:

- Maxwell employed a representation of “hydraulic analogy” proposed by Faraday.

### The incremental character of discovery

> … a theory of scientific discovery is a set of laws that shows how each new step of progress arose from the previous step.
> 

In this sense, there is no fundamental difference between SD and any other problem solving?

### About the logic of scientific discovery

> So the task of a scientist (or a discovery program) is not to achieve the impossible goal of inducing a valid general law from a finite body of data. Their task is to induce a law that fits the data, with the hope (buoyed up by history and prior experience) that the generalization thus discovered may retain its validity as new data are obtained and new phenomena observed.
>
